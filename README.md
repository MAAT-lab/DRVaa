# expRouter

Traditional cartography operates under a paradigm of spatial positivism, optimizing routes for functional efficiency and physical effects while ignoring the city’s affective reality, the pre-cognitive emotional resonances, sensory atmospheres, and somatic micro-experiences that define human territoriality. This project introduces an experimental technological device designed to map urban affect, uncovering hidden spatial dimensions. It serves as a new epistemic tool, positioning new discoveries in artificial intelligence and, more specifically, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as proxy sensors capable of translating qualitative urban atmospheres into navigable graph structures. 

Methodologically, this research establishes a decoupled spatial-semantic architecture that extracts fluid, non-visual affective fields from immersive 360-degree street-level imagery. Offline, an asynchronous system processes visual cues to synthesize latent affective tensors, capturing ephemeral dimensions like acoustic isolation, somatic stress, visual harmony, and even olfactory nostalgia. Rather than defining a space strictly by its infrastructure, these vectors model how a territory acts upon the human psyche. These synthesized attributes are spatially indexed onto OpenStreetMap edges. Online, a natural language compiler parses subjective user instructions (e.g., "a path that feels calm and protective") into deterministic graph weights, calculating routes in under 100ms.

The primary research contribution of this artifact lies in its critical challenge to purely geometric navigation. By shifting the routing objective function from spatial efficiency to affective alignment, the project demonstrates how advanced digital tools can capture the subjective essence of communities and territories, through semantic grounding visualizations and proxy triangulation against existing city datasets. It introduces a transformative methodology for research-creation, proving that computational systems can formalize qualitative urban design theories. Ultimately, this technological advancement expands academic capacity to interpret, simulate, and design for cognitive and psychological comfort within the contemporary city.
